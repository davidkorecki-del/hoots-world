## Honest feel note vs Grok's references
Grok's two references (the 54s lifestyle Reel and the 44s app tour) are built on one real
human moment carried gently, shots held 3–5s, emotion before information. v3 finally works
in that same medium: the faces and hands here are real people on real cameras, with real
light behaving the way real light does — the thing v2's generated actors could never fake.
RA's man genuinely carries the verse: he looks like the end of a hard day, and the turn to
the daylight drive feels earned. RB's heart — one woman, one stream, held across two clips
that are truly the same person in the same place — is the closest either short reel has
come to Grok's single-moment discipline.

Where v3 still falls short of the references, plainly: (1) Grok's moments are bespoke —
shot for the story — while stock arcs are assembled; RA's middle clip keeps a teal night
cast the warm grade only partly tames, and its third beat is green daylight rather than an
evening arrival home. (2) RB opens on weather more than on a person (the umbrella woman
is distant), and it ends inside the crossing rather than in calm — the story resolves in
the words, not the picture. (3) Both reels are ~15s — tighter than Grok's gentle pacing —
because the short-format brief asks for it. Even with those joins, this is the first
version of these two reels that belongs in the same conversation as the references:
nothing in either frame is pretending to be a person.
